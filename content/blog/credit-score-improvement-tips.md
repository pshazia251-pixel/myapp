## Understanding Your Credit Score

Your credit score is a three-digit number (300-850) that affects nearly every financial decision in your life — from mortgage rates to car loans, and even apartment rentals and job applications.

**Credit Score Ranges:**
- 800-850: Exceptional
- 740-799: Very Good
- 670-739: Good
- 580-669: Fair
- 300-579: Poor

## 1. Check Your Credit Reports for Errors

Start by getting your free credit reports from all three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) at AnnualCreditReport.com.

**Common errors to look for:**
- Accounts that don't belong to you
- Incorrect payment statuses
- Wrong credit limits or balances
- Duplicate accounts
- Outdated information

Disputing errors can boost your score by 20-100+ points depending on the severity.

## 2. Pay Down Credit Card Balances

Your **credit utilization ratio** (the percentage of available credit you're using) is the second most important factor in your credit score.

**The goal:** Keep utilization below 30%, ideally under 10%.

**Quick strategy:** If you have a $5,000 credit limit and a $2,500 balance (50% utilization), paying it down to $500 (10% utilization) can significantly boost your score.

## 3. Never Miss a Payment

Payment history is the #1 factor in your credit score, accounting for 35% of the total.

**Tips to never miss a payment:**
- Set up automatic minimum payments on all accounts
- Create payment reminders on your phone
- Pay bills as soon as they arrive
- If you miss a payment, pay it before 30 days to avoid it hitting your credit report

## 4. Become an Authorized User

Ask a family member or trusted friend with excellent credit to add you as an authorized user on their credit card. Their positive payment history can boost your score.

**Important:** Make sure the card issuer reports authorized user activity to the credit bureaus.

## 5. Don't Close Old Accounts

The length of your credit history matters. Closing old accounts can:
- Shorten your average account age
- Reduce your total available credit (increasing utilization)

**Better approach:** Keep old accounts open even if you don't use them regularly. Use them occasionally for small purchases to keep them active.

## 6. Limit New Credit Applications

Each hard inquiry can lower your score by 5-10 points. Limit new credit applications to only when necessary.

**Exception:** Rate shopping for mortgages or auto loans within a 14-45 day window counts as a single inquiry.

## 7. Diversify Your Credit Mix

Having a mix of credit types (credit cards, installment loans, mortgage) shows lenders you can manage different types of debt. This accounts for about 10% of your score.

## How Fast Can You See Results?

- **30 days:** Correcting errors and paying down balances
- **60-90 days:** Building payment history and reducing utilization
- **6-12 months:** Significant improvement with consistent habits
